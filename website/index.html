TRACK: TRANSPARENCY & ACCOUNTABILITY

Verify, don't centralize.

A-Road lets institutions in one African country confirm a record issued by another — without either country handing over its database. Inspired by Estonia's X-Road.

Try the live demo View the repo

The problem

A nurse trained in Kenya. A hospital in Rwanda. No fast way to say yes.

The information exists

Her diploma is genuine. Kenya's registry has the record. What's missing is a way for Rwanda to ask, and get a trustworthy answer.

54 disconnected systems

Every African country runs its own civil registries, education systems, and business registries — none of them talk to each other.

She pays the cost

Without a shared verification layer, she re-proves herself from scratch, or simply isn't believed.

How it works

Three layers. Sovereignty preserved, verification shared.

1

Sovereignty Layer

Each country keeps its own registry. An adapter maps its format to one shared schema — no database is ever handed over.

2

Exchange Layer

A verifier sends a signed query. The issuing country answers with its own Ed25519 digital signature — asymmetric, so only they can sign.

3

Access Layer

Web portal, USSD, or SMS return a plain answer: valid, issued by whom, confirmed when.
